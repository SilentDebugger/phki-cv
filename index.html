Maya Ellis — AI Governance Research

Professional profile

Senior AI Governance Research Scientist with a PhD in Computer Science and more than seven years of postdoctoral and professional research experience examining the ethical, social, and regulatory implications of artificial intelligence. My work connects algorithmic fairness, empirical social science, public policy, and institutional accountability. I develop independent research agendas that produce peer-reviewed scholarship and practical recommendations for organizations adopting high-impact AI systems.

My research evaluates how automated decisions affect individuals, communities, and public institutions. I investigate discriminatory outcomes, limits of transparency, oversight failures, and the conditions under which governance interventions improve real-world accountability. I combine computational analysis with qualitative fieldwork to understand both model behavior and the institutional settings in which that behavior becomes consequential.

I lead interdisciplinary collaborations involving computer scientists, statisticians, philosophers, legal researchers, and public-policy specialists. My approach starts with a clearly articulated problem, an explicit account of affected stakeholders, and a research design that makes assumptions and uncertainty visible. I translate findings into journal articles, reproducible evaluation tools, policy briefs, and accessible presentations for technical and nontechnical audiences.

Independent Research and Project Leadership

Algorithmic fairness and bias evaluation

Lead studies examining how training data, measurement choices, model objectives, and deployment conditions shape disparities in AI-assisted decisions. Develop evaluation plans covering subgroup error rates, calibration, allocation outcomes, intersectional effects, and the stability of findings under distribution shift. Report statistical uncertainty and explain the limitations of individual fairness metrics rather than treating a single score as sufficient evidence of responsible performance.

Evaluate fairness interventions across the full system lifecycle, including dataset construction, model development, threshold selection, human review, and post-deployment monitoring. Compare mitigation strategies using explicitly stated objectives and document trade-offs between performance, equity, interpretability, and operational constraints. Investigate when observed disparities reflect measurement problems, institutional processes, or model behavior.

Accountability, transparency, and oversight

Investigate which transparency practices support meaningful scrutiny and which primarily create documentation without improving accountability. Study model documentation, audit reports, impact assessments, explanation interfaces, and the distribution of responsibility across development and deployment teams. Assess whether affected individuals can understand, question, and obtain review of consequential decisions.

Develop research on audit independence, access to evidence, human oversight, organizational incentives, and incident response. Examine how accountability mechanisms function in practice, including the responsibilities of senior leadership, technical teams, procurement staff, external assessors, and public authorities.

Research leadership and collaboration

Build research plans with defined hypotheses, milestones, responsibilities, and publication goals. Coordinate interdisciplinary teams, supervise research assistants, manage research budgets, and maintain constructive relationships with external partners. Establish reproducible workflows, shared documentation, and regular methodological review so that projects remain rigorous as they scale.

Support junior colleagues through study-design reviews, analysis feedback, writing workshops, and career mentorship. Facilitate collaboration between researchers with different methodological traditions and ensure that technical, normative, and empirical contributions are integrated rather than treated as separate workstreams.

Quantitative, Qualitative, and Computational Methods

Statistical and computational research

Advanced experience with statistical modeling, hypothesis testing, regression analysis, uncertainty estimation, bootstrap procedures, sensitivity analysis, and causal inference. Design observational studies and controlled evaluations with attention to selection effects, confounding, missing data, measurement validity, and the distinction between association and causation.

Use Python, R, and SQL to construct research datasets, implement evaluation pipelines, analyze experimental results, and create clear visual summaries. Maintain version-controlled analysis code, documented data-processing steps, and reproducible computational environments. Review robustness across alternative specifications and make analytical assumptions explicit in published work.

Evaluate classification and prediction systems using performance and fairness measures appropriate to the application. Analyze subgroup behavior, error asymmetries, calibration, representational harms, and changes in performance across settings. Connect quantitative findings to the decisions and institutional practices that determine their social significance.

Qualitative and mixed-methods studies

Design semi-structured interviews, stakeholder consultations, document studies, surveys, and comparative case analyses. Develop sampling strategies and interview protocols, conduct thematic coding, and synthesize evidence across participants with different relationships to AI systems. Pay close attention to research ethics, informed participation, confidentiality, and the interpretation of contextual evidence.

Combine qualitative findings with computational analysis to explain patterns that aggregate metrics alone cannot resolve. Use interviews to identify implementation constraints, organizational incentives, and overlooked harms; then develop testable hypotheses or additional evaluations. Triangulate evidence and clearly distinguish findings that are broadly supported from those that remain exploratory.

Analytical writing and communication

Communicate complex technical and governance questions through academic articles, policy briefs, research presentations, and executive summaries. Explain statistical limitations without obscuring the practical implications of a result. Adapt the level of detail to the audience while preserving the evidence, uncertainty, and reasoning behind each recommendation.

AI governance and regulatory frameworks

Research expertise includes the EU AI Act, AI risk-management approaches, algorithmic impact assessment, public-sector oversight, organizational AI policies, and the relationship between technical standards and institutional accountability. Study how governance requirements are interpreted, implemented, monitored, and evaluated in different organizational settings.

Examine governance across procurement, development, deployment, and ongoing operation. Research questions include the assignment of responsibility, adequacy of evaluation evidence, independence of assurance processes, effectiveness of human oversight, and the ability of affected people to seek explanation or remedy. Distinguish formal compliance activities from evidence that a governance measure actually reduces harm.

Policy advice and technology partnerships

Advise policymakers, public-interest organizations, and technology teams on responsible AI adoption. Translate research into practical recommendations on system inventories, assessment procedures, fairness evaluation, documentation, monitoring, and escalation. Support decision-makers in identifying where additional evidence, safeguards, or institutional capacity are needed before deployment.

Work with partners to define the decision problem before selecting a technical intervention. Facilitate discussions among policy, engineering, legal, and operational teams, synthesize competing priorities, and communicate what the available evidence does and does not justify. Deliver briefings that remain accessible without reducing complex governance choices to a checklist.
